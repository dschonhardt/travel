### Sites/Things to do

*From an r/JapanTravel itinerary thread (Oct 2026). Commenter picks, not verified yet, so check hours before going.*

- ☐ Koenji — vintage/thrift neighborhood on the JR Chuo Line, a few minutes west of Shinjuku. Less polished (and cheaper) than Shimokitazawa. Work the north-side shotengai (shopping streets); some shops carry real embroidered sukajan jackets if you dig
  - ☐ Kosugi-yu — neighborhood sento (public bathhouse) in Koenji, cheap and tattoo friendly. Go in the evening when locals stop in after work
- ☐ Nakano Broadway — retro shopping mall one stop past Koenji on the Chuo Line, easy to pair with it. Mandarake inside is packed with second-hand manga, figures, retro games and trading cards
- ☐ Game Center Mikado, Takadanobaba — retro arcade that runs regular Street Fighter tournaments, fun to watch
- ☐ Kinji Used Clothing, Harajuku — one of the better thrift shops if you only want one reliable stop
- ☐ The Deep — tiny jazz/soul bar with a grand piano and live singers, a quieter alternative to Golden Gai for a drink

### New neighborhoods near the Ryogoku hotel (Nov 7–11)

*Added Oct 11, 2026. Picked to match past favorites: old-Tokyo streets (Asakusa), stewed-offal izakaya (Hoppy Street), Hitachino-style beer and coffee, retro shopping, and cheap counter food. All are on the Toei Oedo Line or JR Sobu Line from Ryogoku, so 1–4 stops. Hours were checked Oct 2026 unless marked otherwise.*

**Sumo note:** the Kyushu Basho runs Nov 8–22 in Fukuoka, so the sumo stables have left Tokyo. There's no morning practice to watch and nothing on at the Kokugikan. Ryogoku is still good for chanko nabe and the museums.

- ☐ **Ryogoku (home base)**
  - ☐ **Edo-Tokyo Museum** reopened Mar 31, 2026 after four years of renovation, with a bigger walk-through Edo streetscape and more interactive exhibits. Tue–Sun 9:30 AM–5:30 PM (Sat until 7:30 PM), **closed Mondays**. Right next to the hotel
  - ☐ **Sumida Hokusai Museum**: small museum of the *Great Wave* artist, who was born in this neighborhood. 9:30 AM–5:30 PM, **closed Mondays**. ~10 min walk
  - ☐ **Ryogoku Edo Noren**: food hall inside the station building, built around a replica sumo ring. Chanko nabe (Chanko Kirishima), sushi, monja and sake stands. ~10 AM–11 PM, hours vary by shop. Easy first-night or rainy-night dinner
- ☐ **Kuramae** (Oedo Line, 1 stop): "Tokyo's Brooklyn," with old warehouses turned into workshops and cafes, just south of Asakusa. Pairs well with the Asakusa/Kappabashi area
  - ☐ **Dandelion Chocolate**: bean-to-bar factory and cafe; you can watch the chocolate being made
  - ☐ **Kakimori**: build your own notebook in a few minutes, plus custom-mixed ink next door. Good souvenir
  - ☐ **Nui. Hostel & Bar Lounge**: on the Sumida River. Cafe 8 AM–6 PM, then a bar 6 PM–1 AM where travelers and locals mix
- ☐ **Kiyosumi-Shirakawa** (Oedo Line, 2 stops): Tokyo's specialty-coffee neighborhood, with quiet streets and a garden. The closest match to the Hitachino taproom vibe, in coffee form
  - ☐ **Blue Bottle Kiyosumi**: the chain's first shop outside the US, in a converted warehouse. 8 AM–7 PM
  - ☐ **Allpress Espresso** roastery and cafe: New Zealand flat whites in a light-filled warehouse
  - ☐ **Kiyosumi Garden**: Edo-era stroll garden with stepping stones across the pond. Small entry fee (hours not checked)
  - ☐ **Museum of Contemporary Art Tokyo (MOT)** in Kiba Park. 10 AM–6 PM, last entry 30 min before close, **open until 8 PM on Fridays in November**
- ☐ **Monzen-Nakacho** (Oedo Line, 3 stops): old Fukagawa shrine town with good cheap drinking alleys
  - ☐ **Tomioka Hachimangu antique market**: held the 1st, 2nd, 4th and 5th Sundays, ~8 AM–3 PM, cancelled in rain. **Sun Nov 8 is the 2nd Sunday, the Japanese and Asian antiques day.** Strong pick for Nov 8. Not held when the 15th or 28th falls on a Sunday
  - ☐ **Fukagawa Fudo-do**: temple next door, with a hall covered in Sanskrit characters and a fire ritual several times a day (times not checked)
  - ☐ Tachinomi (standing bars) and izakaya along the shrine approach. Same vibe as Hoppy Street, with fewer tourists. Try **Fukagawa-meshi** (clam rice), the local dish
- ☐ **Morishita** (Oedo Line, 1 stop, or ~15 min walk)
  - ☐ **Yamariki Honkan**: famous nikomi (offal stew) simmered in Hatcho miso and port wine, made from the same pot for 40+ years. **The Hoppy Street stew, but the legendary version.** Mon–Sat 5:00–10:30 PM, **closed Sundays and holidays**. Reservations Mon–Fri for 2+ before 6 PM; no reservations Sat. Tel 03-3633-1638. The Shinkan branch is permanently closed
- ☐ **Kinshicho and Kameido** (JR Sobu Line, 1–2 stops)
  - ☐ **Kameido Gyoza Honten**: one dish only, plates of small crispy gyoza. Cheap, fast and a local legend. 11 AM–8 PM or until sold out, **closed Tuesdays**
  - ☐ **Kameido Tenjin Shrine**: 1646 shrine with a turtle pond, arched bridges and a view of Skytree. Famous for spring wisteria, but nice and quiet in November
  - ☐ Kinshicho has a dense, unpolished izakaya and bar scene south of the station. A good "Shinjuku at night without the crowds" option close to the hotel (not researched in detail)
- ☐ **Tsukishima** (Oedo Line, 4 stops): **Monja Street**, with dozens of monjayaki shops (Tokyo's runny cousin of okonomiyaki, cooked on your table's griddle). Good group dinner with Jeff and Adam. Pair it with a walk to Tsukudajima's old fishing-village lanes (not checked)

### New neighborhoods farther out, matched to past favorites

*Not checked yet, so confirm hours before going.*

- ☐ **Yanaka / Nezu / Sendagi ("Yanesen")** (JR to Nippori, ~20 min): **if you liked Asakusa.** Old-Tokyo district that largely survived the 1923 earthquake and the WWII bombing. Yanaka Ginza shopping street (menchi-katsu croquettes, "cat street"), Yanaka Cemetery, temple lanes, and Nezu Shrine's torii tunnel. Much quieter than Asakusa
- ☐ **Shibamata** (Keisei Line, ~35–40 min): **if you liked Asakusa and retro arcades.** A Showa-era shopping street (kusa-dango, eel) leads to **Taishakuten temple**, which has amazing carved wood panels. **Shibamata Haikara Yokocho** has retro pinball, old candy and old-fashioned games. The setting of the *Tora-san* films. Good half day
- ☐ **Kappabashi Kitchen Street** (between Ueno and Asakusa): restaurant-supply street with knives, ceramics and plastic food samples. Good for a Japanese knife, since the Sakai knife workshop is on the Osaka leg without Dan
- ☐ **Jimbocho** (near Kanda, where Yuzu Lab is): **if you liked the Kanda ramen spot.** Tokyo's used-bookstore district, and also its curry capital. Stops: Bondy (European-style curry) and Kitchen Nankai (katsu curry)
- ☐ **Kichijoji** (JR Chuo Line, past Koenji): **if you liked Omoide Yokocho.** **Harmonica Yokocho** is a tight grid of tiny bars and stalls. Add **Inokashira Park** (rowboats, autumn color by mid-Nov) and Satou's menchi-katsu. Easy to chain with Koenji and Nakano Broadway into one Chuo Line day
- ☐ **Ningyocho** (one Hanzomon Line stop past Kiyosumi-Shirakawa): Edo-era merchant street with **Amazake Yokocho** (taiyaki, ningyo-yaki cakes, old shops). A short, easy add-on to a Kiyosumi coffee morning

**Suggested pairings for the open Tokyo days:**
- **Sun Nov 8:** Tomioka Hachimangu antique market (Monzen-Nakacho) in the morning → Kiyosumi-Shirakawa coffee and garden → Kuramae in the afternoon. Monja in Tsukishima or chanko in Ryogoku for dinner. Yamariki is closed Sundays
- **Tue Nov 10:** Edo-Tokyo Museum in the morning, Yanesen or Shibamata in the afternoon, Yamariki nikomi for dinner. Kameido Gyoza is closed Tuesdays
- **Wed Nov 11 (departure):** Kiyosumi coffee or Kuramae, then the Asakusa Line to HND. Both are on the airport route

### Yokocho / izakaya alleys (like Omoide Yokocho)

*Added Oct 11, 2026. Alleys with the same tiny-counter, yakitori-smoke feel. Checked against 2026 alley guides, but individual bars weren't checked. Omoide Yokocho itself is under redevelopment, and Shibuya Station construction surrounds Nonbei Yokocho, so check both before going.*

**Closest match:**
- ☐ **Nonbei Yokocho ("Drunkard's Alley"), Shibuya**: postwar alley by the tracks, with a few dozen bars that seat ~6 each. Fewer crowds and lower prices than Omoide Yokocho. Fits the Oct 30 Shibuya stop
- ☐ **Harmonica Yokocho, Kichijoji**: small grid of tiny bars and stalls (see above). Chain it with Koenji and Nakano Broadway on one Chuo Line day
- ☐ **Ameyoko / Ueno–Okachimachi under the tracks**: loud market street by day, standing bars and yakitori spilling onto the street at night. ~15 min from Ryogoku (Sobu Line to Akihabara, then north). **Easiest from the second hotel**

**Under the tracks (gado-shita):**
- ☐ **Yurakucho**: old izakaya in the brick arches under the JR line, with salarymen and yakitori smoke. **Sanchoku Yokocho** nearby is a renovated 2019 alley of 11 bars, each focused on one region's food and ingredients
- ☐ **Kanda**: the same under-the-tracks style near Yuzu Lab and Hitachino. Easy to crawl before or after the Oct 28 dinner

**Polished version:**
- ☐ **Ebisu Yokocho**: ~20 izakaya stalls in a renovated indoor building. Lively and cleaner, less old-Tokyo

**Near Ryogoku:** Monzen-Nakacho standing bars, Kinshicho izakaya, and Yamariki in Morishita. See [New neighborhoods near the Ryogoku hotel](#new-neighborhoods-near-the-ryogoku-hotel-nov-711).

**Best from Ryogoku:** Ueno/Okachimachi or Yurakucho. Both are a short ride away and the closest to the Omoide Yokocho feel.

### More finds for the open end-block days (Nov 7–11)

*Added Oct 16, 2026. Not already in this file, and each one matches a past favorite. Hours for the bars and sento come from listing sites, not the venues, so check before going.*

- ☐ **Tori-no-Ichi fair, Ootori Shrine + Chokokuji (Senzoku, north of Asakusa)**: **if you liked Asakusa at night.** Old-Tokyo "rooster day" fair, ~100 stalls selling **kumade** (decorated bamboo good-luck rakes) and ~800 food stalls. Runs midnight to midnight on **Sat Nov 7** and Thu Nov 19, 2026 (no third rooster day this year). Free. **Fits the evening of Nov 7, after check-in.** Full notes in [Festivals/Events](#festivalsevents-during-our-dates-oct-2730--nov-911)
- ☐ **Beer Club Popeye, Ryogoku** (2-18-7 Ryogoku, 1–2 min from JR Ryogoku): **if you liked Hitachino Brewing Lab.** One of Tokyo's original craft beer bars, with 70–100 taps (mostly Japanese) and some house beer brewed nearby. Happy hour 5–8 PM. **Closed Sundays.** Sources disagree on Mondays. Roughly Mon–Fri 5 PM–midnight, Sat from 3 PM. Busy, so reserve (03-3633-2120)
- ☐ **Daikokuyu sento** (3-12-14 Yokokawa, Sumida, between Kinshicho and Oshiage): **if you liked the Kosugi-yu idea.** Tattoo-friendly neighborhood bath, ~¥550. Open into the next morning (to ~10 AM): Mon and Wed–Fri from 3 PM, Sat from 2 PM, Sun from 1 PM. **Closed Tuesdays.** Best after the Tsukuba hike on Mon Nov 9, or Sun Nov 8
- ☐ **Tsukiji Outer Market** (Oedo Line to Tsukijishijo, ~15 min): **if you liked gyudon breakfasts and counter food.** Grazing breakfast: tamagoyaki, seafood skewers, donburi. Go 7–11 AM, since most shops wind down by ~2 PM. **Closed Sundays and some Wednesdays**, so Tue Nov 10 is the day, not Nov 8 or 11
- ☐ **Akihabara Hokoten**: Chuo-dori closes to cars on Sundays, **1–5 PM Oct–Mar**, from Manseibashi to Sotokanda 5-chome. Cancelled in bad weather. Fits **Sun Nov 8** for a busier, more fun Akihabara
- ☐ **Akabane senbero bars** (JR Saikyo/Utsunomiya Line, ~30 min): **if you liked Hoppy Street.** "Senbero" means getting drunk on ¥1,000. Standing bars that open from noon:
  - ☐ **Tachinomi Ikoi** (main store, ~3 min from the station): old-school and full of regulars, busy even at weekday lunch. Beer ~¥390, motsuni (offal stew) ~¥110
  - ☐ **Maruken Suisan**: oden shop with a standing counter. Listed as 10 AM–9 PM, closed the 3rd Wed of the month (listing may be out of date)
  - ☐ **Sakura Shoten 603**: 24-hour standing bar ~1 min from the station, with a ¥100 menu
- ☐ **Kita-Senju** (Hibiya Line, ~25 min): **if you liked Omoide Yokocho.** **Nomiya Yokocho** is a Showa-era drinking alley that hasn't been swamped by tourists, plus **Beer-Ma**, a standing bar for bottled craft beer (hours not checked)
- ☐ **Sangenjaya: Sankaku Chitai** (Tokyu Den-en-toshi Line, ~45 min, farthest on this list): **if you liked Golden Gai.** Postwar black-market triangle of tiny bars and yakitori, mostly a younger local crowd and few tourists. **Yakushu Bar** is a 6-seat herbal-spirits counter behind a small door with a green light. Only worth it if you're on the west side anyway

**Day fit:**

- **Sun Nov 8:** Akihabara Hokoten in the afternoon, Daikokuyu in the evening. Popeye and Yamariki are closed
- **Mon Nov 9:** Daikokuyu after Tsukuba. Popeye only if it turns out to be open Mondays
- **Tue Nov 10:** Tsukiji breakfast, then senbero in Akabane in the afternoon or Popeye in the evening. Daikokuyu is closed

### Adam's picks

*Places Adam wants to go. Looked up Oct 23, 2026 on listing sites, not the venues' own pages, so check hours before going. Almost all of them are on the west side, so they're grouped into Sun Nov 8 (Shinjuku/Shimokitazawa/Shibuya/Ebisu) and Tue Nov 10 (Chuo Line).*

- ☐ **Nogata Hope** — ramen. Flagship at 2-58-4 Nogata, Nakano (Nogata Station on the Seibu Shinjuku Line, or ~15 min walk north from JR Koenji). Tonkotsu with a triple soup base, classic 元 (Gen) ¥950 ([official menu](https://www.nogata-hope.com/menu), Oct 25). Cash or PayPay only. Main shop 11 AM–3 AM, no closing day ([official](https://www.nogata-hope.com/store)). Nakano branch (2-2-1 Arai, by Nakano Broadway) 11 AM–1:30 AM (Google Maps). **Tue Nov 10**, with Koenji
- ☐ **Udon Shin** — 2-20-16 Yoyogi, Shibuya (~5 min from Shinjuku South Exit). 11 AM–10 PM, closes when the udon runs out. Queues of 1–3 hrs without a booking. **TableCheck reservation is ¥2,000 pp, non-refundable**, and no-shows are ¥3,000 pp. Cash only. **Sun Nov 8 lunch**
- ☐ **Yakitori no Meimon Akiyoshi** — Fukui yakitori chain. **Use the Nakano branch** (5-54-3 Nakano, 03-3385-0071): open daily, weekdays 17:00–22:30, weekends 16:00–22:00. The Ogikubo branch isn't on the [official store list](https://www.akiyoshi.co.jp/shop/tokyo/index.html), and **Shinkoiwa is closed Tuesdays**. Other Tokyo branches: Ueno, Ikebukuro, Shibuya, Ginza, Kanda, Korakuen. **Tue Nov 10 dinner**
- ☐ **Shimokita Senrogai Open Space** (Shimokita Senrogai Akichi) — open lot on the old rail line in Shimokitazawa, with a rotating line-up of food trucks plus the permanent Akichi Cafe & Bar. Truck hours aren't posted, so check their Instagram that week. **Sun Nov 8**, with Shimokitazawa thrift shops and Bonus Track
- ☐ **Kanoya, Shinjuku** — Adam might go solo ("it's nothing special at all lol"). Cheap standing soba/udon counter by Shinjuku's south-east exit (Kanoya Shinjuku Tonanguchi). Hours not found. A quick stop before Udon Shin on Nov 8 or whenever passing Shinjuku
- ☐ **Weird Japanese pizza** — corn mayo or something similar. **Pizza-La** and **Domino's Japan** both do mayo, corn, teriyaki-chicken and potato pizzas. Easiest as delivery to the APA after the Tsukuba hike on **Mon Nov 9**
- ☐ **Sapporo Beer Factory** = **Sapporo Beer Chiba Brewery**, 2 Takasecho, Funabashi (Adam's [Google Maps link](https://maps.app.goo.gl/jtcx5PQhByGmaA1D7); not Yebisu Brewery Tokyo). **No tours**: Sapporo's official brewery page lists Chiba under "other factories (no factory tours)" ([official](https://www.sapporobeer.jp/brewery/)). The only visitor option is the **Sapporo Beer Chiba Beer Garden** on the factory grounds, a Tokyo Bay–view beer restaurant run by Ginza Lion. Per Tabelog: jingisukan Lamb Set (6 cuts), wagyu yakiniku and seafood hamayaki sets, a 100-min all-you-can-drink with Black Label, and an all-you-can-eat jingisukan option. Online booking only (phones closed), 2-hr seating, 100% fee for same-day cancels. Google hours: **Mon–Thu 12–9 PM, Fri 12–10 PM, Sat 11 AM–10 PM, Sun 11 AM–9 PM**. Online reservations via Tabelog / Hot Pepper / Gurunavi. Free first-come shuttle bus (~50 seats) from JR Shin-Narashino (~10 min), Keisei Tsudanuma (~20 min) or JR Tsudanuma (~25 min); timetable not checked. Tsudanuma is on the Sobu local line straight from Ryogoku (~35–40 min). Open every day, so it fits Mon Nov 9 or Tue Nov 10 evening
- ☐ **Yebisu Brewery Tokyo** (separate from Adam's pick) — Sapporo's Yebisu brand at Yebisu Garden Place in Ebisu: museum and taproom free, tastings ~¥1,100, paid tours (Japanese only, book ahead) and an English audio guide. Weekdays 12–8 PM, **weekends 11 AM–7 PM, closed Tuesdays**, no cash. **Sun Nov 8**
- ☐ **Beer Boy** — when in Shibuya Parco. Standing craft beer bar in Shibuya Parco B1, ~10 rotating taps. 11 AM–11:30 PM. **Fri Oct 30**, between the Shibuya crossing and the train to Shinjuku

### New finds for the end block (Oct 23)

*Not already in this file. Checked Oct 23, 2026 against guides, not official pages.*

- ☐ **Shimokitazawa**: not done on past trips. Thrift and vintage shops, small live-music bars, plus **Bonus Track** and **Reload**, two low-rise shopping strips built along the old buried rail line. Senrogai (Adam's food trucks) sits between them. ~8 min from Shinjuku on the Odakyu Line

Runs Oct 31, 2026–Jan 10, 2027, **only on non-racing days**, 4:30–9 PM (last entry 8 PM). Last season's tickets were ~¥800 on weekdays and ~¥1,300–1,600 on weekends.   - **What it is:** an Edo-era merchants' fair for luck and business success, held on the "rooster days" of November. Stalls sell **kumade**, bamboo rakes loaded with masks, coins and gold that "rake in" fortune. When someone buys one, the stall crew does a loud rhythmic hand-clap (**tejime**) to seal the deal. Fun to watch, and you don't need to buy or join in
  - **Scale:** ~100 kumade stalls and ~800 food stalls (yakisoba, takoyaki, yakitori, grilled squid, chocolate bananas, hot sake in paper cups). Lanterns lighting up the rakes after dark is the big draw
  - **Hours:** runs nonstop for 24 hours from midnight, opening with a taiko drum. Some stalls near Chokokuji open ~7 PM the night before and keep going to ~2 AM after. Free, no booking
  - **Crowds:** peak is ~7–11 PM. 3–5 PM is calmer but less atmospheric. It's on a Saturday, so expect it to be packed in the evening. Allow 1.5–2 hrs plus queue time to reach the shrine
  - **Getting there from Ryogoku (~25–30 min):** JR Sobu Line to Akihabara, then the Hibiya Line to **Iriya, Exit 3** (~7 min walk). TX Asakusa Station is ~8 min away. Asakusa Line/Ginza Line Asakusa is a 20+ min walk. Traffic restrictions, so no taxis to the door
  - **Tips:** bring cash (small rakes are ~¥1,000–3,000; big business rakes run ¥30,000+). Travel light, since the lanes get packed. It goes ahead in rain unless there's a safety risk
  - **Smaller alternative:** **Hanazono Shrine** in Shinjuku holds its own Tori-no-Ichi the same days, with an eve fair on Nov 6. It's easier for first-timers and pairs with Golden Gai, but it's farther from Ryogoku

### Sites/Things to do

- ☐ Kenrokuen Garden — one of Japan's "three great gardens," best early morning for light/crowds. Yukitsuri (rope-work bracing the pine branches for snow) goes up starting ~Nov 1 each year, so it should already be in place for our Nov 6–7 visit
  - ☐ **Free early entry** — from Nov 1 the garden opens free from ~6:00 AM (last entry ~7:30), but you must be out 15 min before the paid 8:00 opening. Sources disagree on which gates (most say Renchimon and Zuishinzaka), so check. Only worth it to have the garden nearly empty; our Nov 6 plan uses the 8:00 paid entry
  - ☐ **Shigure-tei tea** — reconstructed Maeda-era teahouse inside Kenrokuen: matcha with its own wagashi, ~¥730 (Jan 2026 prefecture price; older listings say ¥800–1,000). 9:00 AM–4:30 PM, last entry 4:00. Fits the Nov 6 8:00–9:20 garden visit
- ☐ Kanazawa Castle Park — adjacent to Kenrokuen, free to walk grounds
  - ☐ **Gyokusen-inmaru Garden** — restored Edo-era strolling garden inside Castle Park, free, often rated alongside Kenrokuen. Guided tour only (~40 min, mostly in Japanese with an English handout). **Reservation required, book at least a day ahead.** In Teramachi, south of the Sai River. **Booking is by phone only** (+81-76-241-0888, English OK; no online or email booking). ### Food

**Crab season — important timing.** Ishikawa's crab season opens **Nov 6** (boats go out at midnight), and the **first auction is ~7:30 PM Nov 6** (the "Kani-1 Grand Prix" at the Kanazawa market, per 2025). Rough weather can push the opening back (2023's slipped to Nov 9). Kanazawa's tourism board says local crab hits Omicho and shops **from Nov 7**, which is our travel day out. So:
- **Nov 4–6 (before local crab):** go to places that serve crab year-round (imported or tank-held live crab) or red snow crab (beni-zuwai), which is in season earlier
- **Sat Nov 7 (opening day for local crab):** do a **crab lunch at Omicho** before an afternoon Shinkansen. This is the one shot at fresh **kobako gani** (female crab with roe, only in season to Dec 29) and blue-tagged **kano gani** (male)

*Crab names:* **Kano gani** (加能ガニ) = male snow crab, blue tag with the landing port on it, big legs and crab miso. **Kobako gani** (香箱ガニ) = small female with inner and outer roe, the local favorite and much cheaper. **Kani-men** = kobako picked and repacked into its shell, served in Kanazawa oden in season.

*Year-round crab (works Nov 4–6):*

- ☐ Ichinokura, Katamachi (海鮮市場料理 いちの蔵) — live crab tank all year (Okhotsk crab before the local season), choose sashimi, charcoal-grilled or steamed. Whole crab ~¥18,000–26,000 by weight; crab-miso shell roast ~¥1,700. **Walk-ins OK**, counter seats. Also Kanazawa oden. 2F APA Katamachi Daisan Bldg, 1-6-13 Katamachi
- ☐ Ajidokoro Takasaki (味処 髙﨑) — kappo restaurant famous for its **crab cream croquette** (~¥800, red snow crab, year-round), plus a live-crab tank where you pick your size. **Reservations required during crab season.** Near Kazuemachi

*Crab from Nov 7 (local season):*

- ☐ Omicho Market crab stalls — **Kawaki Shoten** (川木商店) is the market's oldest crab specialist and boils crab on site; several kaisendon shops add kobako to their bowls once the season opens. Go late morning before stalls sell out
- ☐ Kourin Sushi (Omicho) — mentioned as an affordable way to try kobako gani as sushi
- ☐ Daimyojaya — crab specialist with live blue-tag crab from Kanazawa Port; sells kano gani by the crab, crab kaiseki and crab shabu courses start Nov 7
- ☐ Otome Sushi — serves kobako from Nov 7, but its status is unclear (see the Michelin table)
- ☐ Kappo Takeshi (Sai River) — crab-only courses all season (sashimi + grilled, crab shabu, or premium with both), fixed price ¥20,900–26,400. Reservations recommended. Best fit if we ever do a Kanazawa dinner on/after Nov 7
- ☐ Sake & Shokuyujin Minato — small, friendly bar-restaurant (solo-friendly) with whole boiled crab (~¥15,000) and crab-miso gratin. **Crab needs a reservation at least one day ahead.** 2-8 Kakinokibatake

*Other Kanazawa food:*

- ☐ Omicho Market — kaisendon (seafood rice bowls), Kanazawa's "kitchen." Also nodoguro (blackthroat seaperch) and amaebi (sweet shrimp)
- ☐ Gold leaf — Kanazawa produces ~99% of Japan's gold leaf; gold-leaf ice cream is a local novelty
- ☐ Kanazawa oden — local style with kani-men (in season), kuruma-fu and bai-gai sea snails. Year-round
- ☐ Jibu-ni — duck stewed in a thick sweet-soy broth with wheat gluten, Kanazawa's classic local dish
- ☐ **Kanazawa curry** (added Oct 19, 2026) — local style: very thick, dark, slightly sweet roux over rice, topped with a katsu cutlet, shredded cabbage on the side, eaten with a fork from a steel plate. **Champion Curry** (founded ~1961) is the original and the most popular local chain. Cheap, fast lunch. Its **Omicho branch** is in B1 of Omicho Ichiba-kan, 11:00 AM–4:00 PM, open daily (backup for the Nov 6 lunch). **Kanazawa Curry Laboratory** (金沢カレー研究工房, 27-2 Jikkenmachi, inside Omicho Market, [map](https://maps.app.goo.gl/epQNdzvxeRLiWYpw7)) is the modern, much-recommended option: build your own plate from three curries (get the gold Kanazawa curry), spice 1–10, rice type, and 20+ toppings (tonkatsu ¥400, Noto pork cutlet ¥550). ~4.4–4.5 stars, lines at lunch. 10:00 AM–3:30 PM and 6:00–10:00 PM, **closed Wednesdays**; only one Kanazawa branch. **Planned for the Fri Nov 6 Omicho lunch (11:00)** (see [Nov 6](./days/2026-11-06.md)). **Turban Curry Honten** (Hirosaka 1-1-48, by Korinbo, since 1971) is the other founding chain and rates about the same (Tabelog 4.2 for its head store vs 4.2 for Champion's suburban Nonoichi head store). Its "black curry" gets the most praise from visitors. Weekday hours conflict (11:00–7:00 PM in the newest listings, to 8:30 PM on the shop's page). **Planned for Dan's dinner Wed Nov 4 at ~5:45** (see [Nov 4](./days/2026-11-04.md)). (Go Go Curry sells the same style but reportedly started in Tokyo, so it's less local)
- ☐ **Hanton rice** (added Oct 19, 2026) — Kanazawa's own Western-style dish: omurice (ketchup rice under omelet) topped with fried white fish and tartar sauce. **Grill Otsuka** (2-9-15 Katamachi) is where it started; reviews are mixed but the portions are big. English-language info is thin, so look it up on a map when there. Fits the **Fri Nov 6 8:30 PM** drinks slot, before or instead of TRI STAR/CHE

Cheap and quick, and reviewers like it. One shop, not a local tradition (Kanazawa isn't actually known for hot dogs). 10:00 AM–5:00 PM, **closed Thursdays** (Google Maps). ### Day Trips

*Only Nov 5–6 are full days, and Nov 5 is Dan solo until Jeff and Adam arrive. So a half-day trip on Nov 5 fits best.*

- ☐ **Gokayama (Ainokura)** — the quieter UNESCO gassho-zukuri village, a good substitute since we skipped Shirakawa-go. Hokuriku Shinkansen to Shin-Takaoka (~13 min), then the World Heritage Bus (~1 hr). Buses are infrequent, so check the timetable both ways. A day bus tour from Kanazawa Station also covers Gokayama + Shirakawa-go (8:00 AM–3:45 PM)
- ☐ **Kaga Onsen** — hot-spring towns south of Kanazawa: ~20 min by Hokuriku Shinkansen to Kaga Onsen Station (~40 min on the IR Ishikawa local). The **CAN Bus** loops from the station (Mountain route: Natadera, Yamashiro, Yamanaka; Sea route: Katayamazu and the coast). **1-day pass price unclear**: the official sheet says ¥1,000, our Nov 5 notes say ¥1,100, so check at the station. Buses are infrequent, so plan around the timetable. **Planned for Thu Nov 5** (see [days/2026-11-05.md](./days/2026-11-05.md)). Public baths are nude, and the one private day-use bath (Ohanami Kyubei, Yamanaka) is closed Thursdays
  - ☐ **Natadera Temple** — halls built into rock cliffs, caves, a pagoda and a garden known for autumn color (early-mid color in early Nov)
  - ☐ **Yamashiro Onsen** — bathhouse square, **Rosanjin's Iroha Soan** (cottage where the potter-chef Rosanjin lived), **Kutani pottery** shops (Yamashiro is Kutani ware's hometown)
    - ☐ **Kosoyu** — reconstructed Meiji-era public bathhouse on the square. Worth seeing from outside (bathing is nude)
    - ☐ **Kutani Porcelain Art Museum** — history of Kutani ware, if the pottery shops hook you
  - ☐ **Yamanaka Onsen** — **Kakusenkei Gorge** walk along the Daishoji River (foliage early-mid Nov), from Kurotani Bridge to the cypress **Korogi Bridge**. Then **Yuge Kaido**, the main street, for Yamanaka lacquerware shops and snacks
    - ☐ **Ayatori Bridge** — red-purple S-shaped bridge on the same gorge walk; make sure the walk reaches it
    - ☐ **Woodturning workshop** (e.g. Mokume) — make your own bowl in the Yamanaka lacquerware tradition
  - ☐ **Yunokuni no Mori** — traditional crafts village with gold-leaf, pottery and lacquer workshops. Entry ~¥550, workshops extra (unconfirmed)
  - ☐ **Katayamazu Onsen** (Sea route, opposite direction) — houseboat cruises on Lake Shibayamagata and a glass-walled lakeside bathhouse with a café. A separate outing, not an add-on to the Mountain route
- ☐ **Eiheiji + Tojinbo (Fukui)** — Eiheiji is a working Zen training temple (founded 1244), 70+ buildings up a cedar-forest hillside; Tojinbo is dramatic basalt sea cliffs. Shinkansen to Fukui ~25 min, then bus. Full day if doing both
- ☐ **Fukui Prefectural Dinosaur Museum (Katsuyama)** — one of the world's big dinosaur museums. Fukui + local train/bus. Full day, pairs with Eiheiji
- ☐ **Takaoka** — on the way to Gokayama: Zuiryuji (National Treasure Zen temple), the Takaoka Great Buddha, and the Amaharashi coast with views of the Tateyama range across the bay on a clear day
- ☐ **Noto Peninsula (Wajima)** — still rebuilding after the 2024 earthquake. The Wajima morning market reopened at a temporary site (Wai Wai Plaza, 8 AM–noon, closed 2nd/4th Wed). ~2–2.5 hrs each way, so it's really an overnight trip; not realistic for us this time
