### New neighborhoods near the Ryogoku hotel (Nov 7–11)

*Added Oct 11, 2026. Picked to match past favorites: old-Tokyo streets (Asakusa), stewed-offal izakaya (Hoppy Street), Hitachino-style beer and coffee, retro shopping, and cheap counter food. All are on the Toei Oedo Line or JR Sobu Line from Ryogoku, so 1–4 stops. Hours were checked Oct 2026 unless marked otherwise.*

**Sumo note:** the Kyushu Basho runs Nov 8–22 in Fukuoka, so the sumo stables have left Tokyo. There's no morning practice to watch and nothing on at the Kokugikan. Ryogoku is still good for chanko nabe and the museums.

- ☐ **Ryogoku (home base)**
  - ☐ **Edo-Tokyo Museum** reopened Mar 31, 2026 after four years of renovation, with a bigger walk-through Edo streetscape and more interactive exhibits. Tue–Sun 9:30 AM–5:30 PM (Sat until 7:30 PM), **closed Mondays**. Right next to the hotel
  - ☐ **Sumida Hokusai Museum**: small museum of the *Great Wave* artist, who was born in this neighborhood. 9:30 AM–5:30 PM, **closed Mondays**. ~10 min walk
  - ☐ **Ryogoku Edo Noren**: food hall inside the station building, built around a replica sumo ring. Chanko nabe (Chanko Kirishima), sushi, monja and sake stands. ~10 AM–11 PM, hours vary by shop. Easy first-night or rainy-night dinner
- ☐ **Kuramae** (Oedo Line, 1 stop): "Tokyo's Brooklyn," with old warehouses turned into workshops and cafes, just south of Asakusa. Pairs well with the Asakusa/Kappabashi area
  - ☐ **Dandelion Chocolate**: bean-to-bar factory and cafe; you can watch the chocolate being made
  - ☐ **Kakimori**: build your own notebook in a few minutes, plus custom-mixed ink next door. Good souvenir
  - ☐ **Nui. Hostel & Bar Lounge**: on the Sumida River. Cafe 8 AM–6 PM, then a bar 6 PM–1 AM where travelers and locals mix
- ☐ **Kiyosumi-Shirakawa** (Oedo Line, 2 stops): Tokyo's specialty-coffee neighborhood, with quiet streets and a garden. The closest match to the Hitachino taproom vibe, in coffee form
  - ☐ **Blue Bottle Kiyosumi**: the chain's first shop outside the US, in a converted warehouse. 8 AM–7 PM
  - ☐ **Allpress Espresso** roastery and cafe: New Zealand flat whites in a light-filled warehouse
  - ☐ **Kiyosumi Garden**: Edo-era stroll garden with stepping stones across the pond. Small entry fee (hours not checked)
  - ☐ **Museum of Contemporary Art Tokyo (MOT)** in Kiba Park. 10 AM–6 PM, last entry 30 min before close, **open until 8 PM on Fridays in November**
- ☐ **Monzen-Nakacho** (Oedo Line, 3 stops): old Fukagawa shrine town with good cheap drinking alleys
  - ☐ **Tomioka Hachimangu antique market**: held the 1st, 2nd, 4th and 5th Sundays, ~8 AM–3 PM, cancelled in rain. **Sun Nov 8 is the 2nd Sunday, the Japanese and Asian antiques day.** Strong pick for Nov 8. Not held when the 15th or 28th falls on a Sunday
  - ☐ **Fukagawa Fudo-do**: temple next door, with a hall covered in Sanskrit characters and a fire ritual several times a day (times not checked)
  - ☐ Tachinomi (standing bars) and izakaya along the shrine approach. Same vibe as Hoppy Street, with fewer tourists. Try **Fukagawa-meshi** (clam rice), the local dish
- ☐ **Morishita** (Oedo Line, 1 stop, or ~15 min walk)
  - ☐ **Yamariki Honkan**: famous nikomi (offal stew) simmered in Hatcho miso and port wine, made from the same pot for 40+ years. **The Hoppy Street stew, but the legendary version.** Mon–Sat 5:00–10:30 PM, **closed Sundays and holidays**. Reservations Mon–Fri for 2+ before 6 PM; no reservations Sat. Tel 03-3633-1638. The Shinkan branch is permanently closed
- ☐ **Kinshicho and Kameido** (JR Sobu Line, 1–2 stops)
  - ☐ **Kameido Gyoza Honten**: one dish only, plates of small crispy gyoza. Cheap, fast and a local legend. 11 AM–8 PM or until sold out, **closed Tuesdays**
  - ☐ **Kameido Tenjin Shrine**: 1646 shrine with a turtle pond, arched bridges and a view of Skytree. Famous for spring wisteria, but nice and quiet in November
  - ☐ Kinshicho has a dense, unpolished izakaya and bar scene south of the station. A good "Shinjuku at night without the crowds" option close to the hotel (not researched in detail)
- ☐ **Tsukishima** (Oedo Line, 4 stops): **Monja Street**, with dozens of monjayaki shops (Tokyo's runny cousin of okonomiyaki, cooked on your table's griddle). Good group dinner with Jeff and Adam. Pair it with a walk to Tsukudajima's old fishing-village lanes (not checked)

### New neighborhoods farther out, matched to past favorites

*Not checked yet, so confirm hours before going.*

- ☐ **Yanaka / Nezu / Sendagi ("Yanesen")** (JR to Nippori, ~20 min): **if you liked Asakusa.** Old-Tokyo district that largely survived the 1923 earthquake and the WWII bombing. Yanaka Ginza shopping street (menchi-katsu croquettes, "cat street"), Yanaka Cemetery, temple lanes, and Nezu Shrine's torii tunnel. Much quieter than Asakusa
- ☐ **Shibamata** (Keisei Line, ~35–40 min): **if you liked Asakusa and retro arcades.** A Showa-era shopping street (kusa-dango, eel) leads to **Taishakuten temple**, which has amazing carved wood panels. **Shibamata Haikara Yokocho** has retro pinball, old candy and old-fashioned games. The setting of the *Tora-san* films. Good half day
- ☐ **Kappabashi Kitchen Street** (between Ueno and Asakusa): restaurant-supply street with knives, ceramics and plastic food samples. Good for a Japanese knife, since the Sakai knife workshop is on the Osaka leg without Dan
- ☐ **Jimbocho** (near Kanda, where Yuzu Lab is): **if you liked the Kanda ramen spot.** Tokyo's used-bookstore district, and also its curry capital. Stops: Bondy (European-style curry) and Kitchen Nankai (katsu curry)
- ☐ **Kichijoji** (JR Chuo Line, past Koenji): **if you liked Omoide Yokocho.** **Harmonica Yokocho** is a tight grid of tiny bars and stalls. Add **Inokashira Park** (rowboats, autumn color by mid-Nov) and Satou's menchi-katsu. Easy to chain with Koenji and Nakano Broadway into one Chuo Line day
- ☐ **Ningyocho** (one Hanzomon Line stop past Kiyosumi-Shirakawa): Edo-era merchant street with **Amazake Yokocho** (taiyaki, ningyo-yaki cakes, old shops). A short, easy add-on to a Kiyosumi coffee morning

**Suggested pairings for the open Tokyo days:**
- **Sun Nov 8:** Tomioka Hachimangu antique market (Monzen-Nakacho) in the morning → Kiyosumi-Shirakawa coffee and garden → Kuramae in the afternoon. Monja in Tsukishima or chanko in Ryogoku for dinner. Yamariki is closed Sundays
- **Tue Nov 10:** Edo-Tokyo Museum in the morning, Yanesen or Shibamata in the afternoon, Yamariki nikomi for dinner. Kameido Gyoza is closed Tuesdays
- **Wed Nov 11 (departure):** Kiyosumi coffee or Kuramae, then the Asakusa Line to HND. Both are on the airport route

### Yokocho / izakaya alleys (like Omoide Yokocho)

*Added Oct 11, 2026. Alleys with the same tiny-counter, yakitori-smoke feel. Checked against 2026 alley guides, but individual bars weren't checked. Omoide Yokocho itself is under redevelopment, and Shibuya Station construction surrounds Nonbei Yokocho, so check both before going.*

**Closest match:**
- ☐ **Nonbei Yokocho ("Drunkard's Alley"), Shibuya**: postwar alley by the tracks, with a few dozen bars that seat ~6 each. Fewer crowds and lower prices than Omoide Yokocho. Fits the Oct 30 Shibuya stop
- ☐ **Harmonica Yokocho, Kichijoji**: small grid of tiny bars and stalls (see above). Chain it with Koenji and Nakano Broadway on one Chuo Line day
- ☐ **Ameyoko / Ueno–Okachimachi under the tracks**: loud market street by day, standing bars and yakitori spilling onto the street at night. ~15 min from Ryogoku (Sobu Line to Akihabara, then north). **Easiest from the second hotel**

**Under the tracks (gado-shita):**
- ☐ **Yurakucho**: old izakaya in the brick arches under the JR line, with salarymen and yakitori smoke. **Sanchoku Yokocho** nearby is a renovated 2019 alley of 11 bars, each focused on one region's food and ingredients
- ☐ **Kanda**: the same under-the-tracks style near Yuzu Lab and Hitachino. Easy to crawl before or after the Oct 28 dinner

**Polished version:**
- ☐ **Ebisu Yokocho**: ~20 izakaya stalls in a renovated indoor building. Lively and cleaner, less old-Tokyo

**Near Ryogoku:** Monzen-Nakacho standing bars, Kinshicho izakaya, and Yamariki in Morishita. See [New neighborhoods near the Ryogoku hotel](#new-neighborhoods-near-the-ryogoku-hotel-nov-711).

**Best from Ryogoku:** Ueno/Okachimachi or Yurakucho. Both are a short ride away and the closest to the Omoide Yokocho feel.

### Day Trips

*Not done on the 2023 or 2025 trips — candidates for the Oct 27–30 or Nov 9–11 blocks. Autumn foliage timing noted where it's a real factor for our dates; double-check closer to the trip since peak dates vary year to year.*

**Hiking:**

- ☐ Nikko — Tobu Nikko Line limited express direct from Asakusa (~2 hrs), convenient since we're staying right there for the first block. Kegon Falls, Lake Chuzenji, and easy lakeside/marshland trails around Senjogahara; Toshogu Shrine (UNESCO) too if mixing in sightseeing. Famous for autumn color — the Irohazaka pass and Chuzenji area typically peak early-to-mid November, which lines up well with the Nov 9–11 block. Long day either way (early out, late back)
- ☐ Okutama / Mount Mitake — ~2 hrs from Shinjuku (Chuo Line to Ome, transfer to Ome Line, then cable car). Cable car up to Musashi-Mitake Shrine, then trails out to Nanayo Falls or on to Mount Odake. Quieter and more forested than Takao, with river/gorge scenery in Okutama town itself. Foliage usually mid-to-late November, so may be a touch early for the Nov 9–11 dates but worth a look
- ☐ Chichibu — Seibu Ikebukuro Line limited express (Red Arrow), ~1.5–2 hrs. Mount Buko or the ridge trails above Hitsujiyama Park for the hike, plus a small, laid-back mountain town with sake breweries below — good hike-and-town combo in one trip. Foliage typically peaks mid-November
- ☐ Mount Tsukuba (Ibaraki) — ~1.5–2 hrs via Tsukuba Express + shuttle bus. Twin-peaked "sacred mountain" with a ropeway and cable car up either side and a connecting ridge trail between the peaks. Less touristy than Takao/Mitake, good views back toward Tokyo on a clear day; foliage typically mid-to-late November

**Small towns/cities:**

- ☐ Kamakura — ~1 hr from Tokyo Station. Great Buddha (Kotoku-in), the bamboo grove at Hokokuji, Hase-dera, the Enoden coastal train line, walkable temple-hopping streets. Easy to extend to Enoshima island (short causeway walk, seafood, shrine, hilltop lookout) for a fuller day
- ☐ Sawara (Chiba) — "Little Edo" canal town via JR Narita Line, ~1.5–2 hrs, noticeably less touristy than Kawagoe (which we already did). Preserved Edo-era merchant houses along the Ono River, sightseeing boats, birthplace of Ino Tadataka (first person to map Japan)
- ☐ Narita — Naritasan Shinshoji temple and the Omotesando approach street (eel restaurants, old shopfronts), ~1 hr via Keisei or JR. Short trip — easy as a half-day or paired with something else
- ☐ Mito (Ibaraki) — ~1–1.5 hrs via JR Joban Line. Kairakuen, one of Japan's "three great gardens" — best known for plum blossoms (wrong season for us) but still a nice, uncrowded garden walk any time of year, in a city center that sees little tourist traffic
- ☐ Atami — hot spring resort town on the coast, ~50 min via Tokaido Shinkansen. Public bathhouses/foot baths, ocean views, Atami Plum Garden — an easy, low-key onsen day trip
- ☐ Odawara — castle town at the foot of Hakone (which we already did in 2025), ~35 min via Shinkansen. Odawara Castle, walkable town center — works as a standalone half-day or tacked onto a second Hakone-area visit

## Nagoya

### Sites/Things to do

- ☐ Nabana no Sato winter illumination — flower park in Kuwana (Mie), ~40 min from Nagoya via Kintetsu train + shuttle bus. 2026–2027 season confirmed to run Oct 17, 2026 – May 31, 2027, so it's on for our Oct 31–Nov 2 dates. Evening light display (8M+ LEDs, gold-light tunnel), plus flower dome/gardens and plenty of seating — good relaxed evening pairing with a Nagoya day
- ☐ Osu — neighborhood in Naka-ku, south of Sakae
  - ☐ Osu Shopping Arcade — covered arcade, food stalls, retro/electronics shops
  - ☐ Osu Kannon Temple
- ☐ City views (Sakae) — Mirai Tower and Oasis 21 are right by each other, easy to pair
  - ☐ Chubu Electric Power MIRAI TOWER (formerly Nagoya TV Tower) — ¥1,300 for the observation decks: indoor Sky Deck at 90m (360° views, ceiling mirror display) + outdoor Sky Balcony at 100m (open-air). Night view is a designated Japan Night View Heritage site; sunset into early evening is the most photographed slot since the tower got LED illumination in 2020. Floors 1–3 (shops/restaurants) are free even without going up
  - ☐ Oasis 21 "Water Spaceship" — free glass rooftop deck, 14m up, 10:00–21:00. Water flows across the glass roof above the walkway; lit up with colored lights at night. Free alternative/pairing to Mirai Tower. **On Halloween (Oct 31) it closes at 5:00 PM** and the shops are closed all day (2025 rules). See Festivals/Events below
- ☐ Toyota Commemorative Museum of Industry and Technology — Toyota's original plant, textile-to-automobile history
- ☐ Hisaya-odori Park — long central park strip through Sakae, right below Nagoya TV Tower; good walk/rest stop between Sakae shopping and dinner
- ☐ Legoland Japan — near the port, big time commitment if going all in
- ☐ Nagoya Castle — Honmaru Palace only; the main keep/tower has been closed to the public since 2018 for seismic reasons (wooden rebuild targeted ~2032). Palace alone is still worth it, just don't expect to go up the tower
- ☐ SCMAGLEV and Railway Park — maglev/shinkansen history museum, good if anyone's into trains
- ☐ Ghibli Park — Nagakute City, ~50–60 min from Nagoya Station (Higashiyama subway to Fujigaoka, transfer to Linimo maglev to Ai-Chikyuhaku-Kinen-Koen), ~¥670–710 one-way. Tickets ¥1,000–7,800, timed-entry, released online on the 10th of each month at 2:00 PM JST (through official Lawson Ticket English site or Klook for international buyers) — need to book well ahead of the trip, not something to plan day-of

### Food

- ☐ Hatcho miso (red miso) — Nagoya/Aichi specialty, made in Okazaki with a centuries-old method; the base for the dishes below, worth trying in some form
  - ☐ Misokatsu (miso-glazed pork cutlet) — local specialty
    - ☐ Yabaton — the well-known misokatsu chain, est. 1947. Flagship/original location is the Yaba-cho Honten in Osu (Naka Ward) — easy to fold into an Osu visit. Also has branches at Nagoya Station (Esca underground mall and the Meitetsu Department Store). Also try the curry bun (per a video recommendation)
  - ☐ Miso nikomi udon — udon simmered in red miso broth, often served in an individual clay pot
  - ☐ Miso oden — oden (daikon, eggs, konjac, fish cakes, tofu simmered in dashi) served with a red miso glaze/dip instead of the usual broth-only style
- ☐ Tebasaki (fried chicken wings) — Nagoya specialty, Furaibo is the well-known chain
- ☐ Uiro — steamed rice flour cake, soft/chewy with delicate sweetness; Nagoya's been a major producer since the Edo period and it's considered a signature local wagashi. Osu Uiro (est. 1949, in the Osu Shopping Arcade) is the famous shop — good to grab while walking through Osu
- ☐ Chita beef steak sushi — Chita beef (知多牛) is a branded wagyu-style beef from the Chita Peninsula south of Nagoya, known for being tender and high in oleic acid; served as seared beef steak nigiri at some sushi/steak spots around the city

### Festivals/Events during our dates (Oct 31–Nov 1)

*Checked Oct 8, 2026. Halloween is Sat Oct 31, our first Nagoya night. Sakae is the costume-and-drinks area, and it's where we already have dinner (Furaibo) that night.*

- ☐ Nagoya Halloween Bar Crawl — Fri Oct 30 and **Sat Oct 31**, Sakae. Pick up a wristband at **Second Home** (B1 Ark Shirakawa Park Bldg, 2-2-23 Sakae), Sat 2:00–11:00 PM. The wristband gets you specials at the participating bars (free entry, free drinks, or all-you-can-drink, depending on the bar). Guided route both nights, costume contest, costumes optional, English-friendly. One night: **¥3,500 pp through Oct 18**, then ¥5,000 until online sales close Oct 29. Easiest fit: join after the 7:30 PM tebasaki dinner. [Tickets](https://aichiconnection.com/halloween-bar-crawl-2026/)
- ☐ Supernatural Halloween — Sat Oct 31, 8:00 PM–5:00 AM, Club MAGO + Live & Lounge VIO. Big costume party with DJs, aimed at the international crowd. 18+ with ID. ¥3,000 advance / ¥4,000 at the door. Pick this if the group wants a club night rather than bar-hopping. [Tickets](https://smallworldjpn.com/supernatural-halloween/)
- ☐ Shooters Sports Bar & Grill — Halloween party Oct 30–31, opens 5:00 PM, event from 7:00 PM, ¥1,500 entry. Low-key American sports bar option
- **Walking around to see costumes:** **Oasis 21** used to be where costumed crowds gathered, but the city now shuts it down for Halloween. In 2025 the rooftop and ground level closed from **5:00 PM on Oct 30–31**, and the shops were closed all day on the 31st. **Hisaya-odori Park** was also restricted from 5:00 PM on the 31st. ### Sake Breweries

Takayama has seven historic breweries, nearly all within a few minutes' walk of each other on Sanmachi Suji / Kami-Ninomachi. That makes it easy to hit several in one afternoon (Mon Nov 2 is the slot in the day plan). Tastings are cheap: free to ~¥500, or pay-per-cup coin machines. Breweries usually close by ~5 PM, so start by ~3:30.

*Checked Oct 2026. Sources disagree on some hours, closed days and tasting prices (noted below), so treat these as approximate. Mon Nov 2 should be fine for all of them. Nov 3 is a holiday and some may be closed.*

- ☐ **Hirase Sake Brewery** (平瀬酒造) — oldest in Gifu, brewing since 1623; brand **Kusudama**. 20+ sakes to taste, three old earthen storehouses out back, and sarubobo-print cups that make good souvenirs. 82 Kamiichinomachi. ~9 AM–5 PM. ⚠️ One source says closed Sundays; tasting listed as free *or* ¥1,000 per 30 min (likely changed recently). Tours ~¥4,000 incl. tasting
- ☐ **Harada Sake Brewery** (原田酒造場) — since 1855; brand **Sansha**. Known for ginjo made with flower yeast. ~¥500 tasting cup gets you ~10 sakes. Get the **daiginjo sake soft serve** (~¥400). Irori hearth seating. 10 Kamisannomachi. ~8:30/9 AM–5/6 PM, open daily. Probably the best one-stop tasting
- ☐ **Funasaka Sake Brewery** (舩坂酒造店) — 200+ years; brands **Miyama Giku**, **Hida no Jingoro**. The biggest, most "theme park" stop: courtyard, shop, an on-site restaurant (Hida beef), and an *ochoko gacha* coin machine for tastings (~¥150/coin). 105 Kamininomachi. Open daily, ~8:30 AM–6 PM (tasting windows may be limited to ~10–12 and 1–4)
- ☐ **Kawashiri Sake Brewery** (川尻酒造場) — small, traditional; brands **Hida Masamune** / **Tenon**. Tiny production, mostly sold only in Takayama, so a good place to buy a bottle you won't find at home. Tasting ~¥500 sake / ¥330 plum wine. 68 Kamininomachi. 8 AM–5 PM, open daily
- ☐ **Hirata Sake Brewery** (平田酒造場) — brand **Suiou** (aged 10+ years, won national awards), also the novelty "Meteor" sake. Paid coin-dispenser tastings (~¥250/coin). Small and chatty, and also runs the Hirata folk museum. 43 Kamininomachi. ⚠️ Closed days conflict (Tuesdays vs weekends/holidays), so plan it for **Mon Nov 2**
- ☐ **Niki Sake Brewery** (二木酒造) — ginjo specialist, **Tama no I** daiginjo. Lovely old machiya building (used for TV filming). Paid tasting. 40 Kamininomachi. 8 AM–5 PM, closes occasionally
- ☐ **Oita Sake Brewery** (老田酒造店) — brand **Onikoroshi** ("demon killer"), a dry sake with a ~300-year history. Free tasting, plus a shop/café in the renovated original building. 67 Kamisannomachi. 9 AM–5 PM, closes occasionally

**Bonus (not sake):** ☐ **Hida Craft** — small fruit-liqueur maker opened 2022 (award-winning **LEMONIQ** lemon liqueur, yuzu cello), free tastings before you buy. Weekdays ~10 AM–4 PM only, call ahead.

**If short on time:** Harada (wide tasting + soft serve), then Funasaka (the big one), then Hirase (the history).

## Kanazawa

### Sites/Things to do

- ☐ Kenrokuen Garden — one of Japan's "three great gardens," best early morning for light/crowds. Yukitsuri (rope-work bracing the pine branches for snow) goes up starting ~Nov 1 each year, so it should already be in place for our Nov 6–7 visit

**More ideas (added Oct 11, 2026):**

- ☐ Myoryuji "Ninja Temple" — Maeda-clan temple full of trick doors, hidden stairs and escape routes. Guided tour only (~40 min, mostly in Japanese with an English handout). **Reservation required, book at least a day ahead.** In Teramachi, south of the Sai River
- ☐ Nishi Chaya + Teramachi — the smaller, quieter geisha district, and right next to it Teramachi, ~70 temples in a few blocks. Pairs with Myoryuji

- ☐ D.T. Suzuki Museum — minimalist Zen museum with a reflecting "water mirror" courtyard. Small and calm, ~45 min. Near the 21st Century Museum
- ☐ Fukumitsuya Sake Brewery — Kanazawa's oldest brewery (1625). English-guided tour with 4 tastings, ~1 hr, **Oct–April, reservation required**

- ☐ Kanazawa Phonograph Museum — small museum of antique gramophones with live listening demos. Near Higashi Chaya

### Food

**Crab season — important timing.** Ishikawa's crab season opens at **midnight Nov 6**, but the boats land the catch overnight and the **first auction is ~3 AM Nov 7**. Kanazawa's tourism board says local crab hits Omicho and shops **from Nov 7**, which is our travel day out. So:
- **Nov 4–6 (before local crab):** go to places that serve crab year-round (imported or tank-held live crab) or red snow crab (beni-zuwai), which is in season earlier
- **Sat Nov 7 (opening day for local crab):** do a **crab lunch at Omicho** before an afternoon Shinkansen. This is the one shot at fresh **kobako gani** (female crab with roe, only in season to Dec 29) and blue-tagged **kano gani** (male)

*Crab names:* **Kano gani** (加能ガニ) = male snow crab, blue tag with the landing port on it, big legs and crab miso. **Kobako gani** (香箱ガニ) = small female with inner and outer roe, the local favorite and much cheaper. **Kani-men** = kobako picked and repacked into its shell, served in Kanazawa oden in season.

*Year-round crab (works Nov 4–6):*

- ☐ Ichinokura, Katamachi (海鮮市場料理 いちの蔵) — live crab tank all year (Okhotsk crab before the local season), choose sashimi, charcoal-grilled or steamed. Whole crab ~¥18,000–26,000 by weight; crab-miso shell roast ~¥1,700. **Walk-ins OK**, counter seats. Also Kanazawa oden. 2F APA Katamachi Daisan Bldg, 1-6-13 Katamachi
- ☐ Ajidokoro Takasaki (味処 髙﨑) — kappo restaurant famous for its **crab cream croquette** (~¥800, red snow crab, year-round), plus a live-crab tank where you pick your size. **Reservations required during crab season.** Near Kazuemachi

*Crab from Nov 7 (local season):*

- ☐ Omicho Market crab stalls — **Kawaki Shoten** (川木商店) is the market's oldest crab specialist and boils crab on site; several kaisendon shops add kobako to their bowls once the season opens. Go late morning before stalls sell out
- ☐ Kourin Sushi (Omicho) — mentioned as an affordable way to try kobako gani as sushi
- ☐ Daimyojaya — crab specialist with live blue-tag crab from Kanazawa Port; sells kano gani by the crab, crab kaiseki and crab shabu courses start Nov 7
- ☐ Otome Sushi — serves kobako from Nov 7, but its status is unclear (see the Michelin table)
- ☐ Kappo Takeshi (Sai River) — crab-only courses all season (sashimi + grilled, crab shabu, or premium with both), fixed price ¥20,900–26,400. Reservations recommended. Best fit if we ever do a Kanazawa dinner on/after Nov 7
- ☐ Sake & Shokuyujin Minato — small, friendly bar-restaurant (solo-friendly) with whole boiled crab (~¥15,000) and crab-miso gratin. **Crab needs a reservation at least one day ahead.** 2-8 Kakinokibatake

*Other Kanazawa food:*

- ☐ Omicho Market — kaisendon (seafood rice bowls), Kanazawa's "kitchen." Also nodoguro (blackthroat seaperch) and amaebi (sweet shrimp)
- ☐ Gold leaf — Kanazawa produces ~99% of Japan's gold leaf; gold-leaf ice cream is a local novelty
- ☐ Kanazawa oden — local style with kani-men (in season), kuruma-fu and bai-gai sea snails. Year-round
- ☐ Jibu-ni — duck stewed in a thick sweet-soy broth with wheat gluten, Kanazawa's classic local dish
