## Plan
All times are rough. The mountain has twin peaks, **Nantai-san** (871 m) and **Nyotai-san** (877 m, the higher one with the better view). They're joined by a short ridge with **Miyukigahara** and its teahouses in the middle. The rain-adjusted plan has three parts: ride the cable car up if the trail is wet, eat lunch at the top while the weather clears, do the summits in the afternoon, then hike down if the trail allows.

| Est. time | What |
|---|---|
| 9:00 AM | Leave the hotel (JR Ryogoku → Akihabara, 2 stops). Grab konbini breakfast and **onigiri/snacks for the trail** as a lunch backup. Bring a rain jacket |
| ~9:20 AM | At Akihabara, tap in with the IC card and take the **TX Rapid** to Tsukuba |
| ~10:05 AM | Arrive at Tsukuba. Last real toilet and konbini stop |
| 10:20 AM | **Shuttle bus** to Tsukubasan-jinja-iriguchi (arrives 10:58). Backup: the 11:00 bus (arrives 11:38) |
| ~11:05 AM | Walk up (~10 min) to **Tsukubasan Shrine**. The mountain itself is the object of worship, so the shrine sits at the base. Its maples and ginkgos peak mid-to-late Nov, so they'll be partly turned |
| ~11:20 AM | **Go up.** If it's still raining or the steps are wet, ride the **cable car** to Miyukigahara (~8 min; hours not rechecked). If it's dry, **hike the Miyukigahara Course** instead: ~2 km, ~610 m of gain, ~1.5 hrs of steep stone steps and roots through cedar forest beside the cable car tracks (top by ~12:50) |
| ~11:40 AM | **Lunch at the Miyukigahara teahouses** (most are 10:00–15:00), the saddle between the peaks. Get **Tsukuba udon**, the local udon with pork and Ibaraki vegetables. Weekday hours can be irregular, which is why you're carrying the konbini backup. A hot bowl indoors is the right way to wait out the last of the rain |
| ~12:30 PM | Side trip to the **Nantai-san summit** (~15 min each way): a small shrine at the top and a **beech forest** that turns gold in early Nov |
| ~1:15 PM | Walk the ridge to **Nyotai-san** (~20 min). The rocky summit has the **best view on the mountain**: the Kanto Plain, Lake Kasumigaura, and Tokyo/Skytree and Fuji on a clear day. The afternoon is when the sky should clear, so this is the part to protect |
| ~1:45 PM | **Hike down the Shirakumobashi Course** back to the shrine (~1.5 hrs). The top section passes the famous **giant rocks**: **Benkei Nanamodori** (a boulder balanced overhead that Benkei supposedly hesitated under seven times), **Mother's Womb Rock**, and others. Then a long quiet forest descent. **If the rocks are slick after the morning rain**, walk back to Miyukigahara and ride the cable car down instead |
| ~3:15 PM | Back at the shrine. Optional snack or souvenir stop on the shrine approach |
| 3:40 or 4:25 PM | Bus from the **Shrine Entrance** back to Tsukuba Center (~40 min). Last bus is **5:15 PM** |
| ~4:30–5:15 PM | TX Rapid back to Akihabara (~45 min) |
| ~5:30–6:00 PM | Back in Tokyo. Shower, dinner near the hotel, then **pack for the flight** |

**Onsen option (instead of hiking down):** after Nyotai-san, walk back to Miyukigahara and take the cable car down. That puts you at the shrine by ~2:00. Both baths are near the shrine, and both are listed as having irregular closing days for maintenance, so phone first:
- **Tsukubasan Edoya** (029-866-0321): 390+ year-old inn, ~5 min walk from the shrine. Entry 11:30, last reception **2:00 PM**, out by 3:00. ¥1,540, hand towel included. With today's later start you'd need to be down by ~1:50, so this one is tight
- **Tsukubasan Hotel Aokiya**: rooftop open-air bath with views out to Skytree and Fuji on a clear day. **12:00–3:00 PM**, ¥1,200. This one fits the later start better

**If the morning is dry after all:** go back to the original early plan. Leave at 7:30 AM and take the 8:50 bus. Hike up the Miyukigahara Course at ~9:50, eat lunch at the top at ~12:00, then do Nyotai-san at ~12:45 and hike down Shirakumobashi.

**If running late:** drop the Nantai-san side trip, since Nyotai-san is the better summit. If it's past ~2:00 when you leave Nyotai, take the cable car or ropeway down instead of hiking.

**If it's still pouring at noon:** the summit view is the payoff, and it won't be there. Consider staying in Tokyo instead. The Ryogoku-area museums (Edo-Tokyo Museum, Sumida Hokusai Museum) are open on Tuesdays.

## Foliage on Nov 10
Color moves down the mountain. Expect the **summit and upper slopes in color** (beech gold, some red maples), the **middle slopes and cable car line around peak**, and the **shrine area still a week or two early** (it peaks mid-to-late Nov). The **Momiji Festival's night cable car and maple light-up** (5–8 PM) runs **weekends and holidays only**, so you won't see it on a Tuesday.
