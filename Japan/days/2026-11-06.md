## Omicho lunch options
**Suggested: Kanazawa Curry Laboratory** (金沢カレー研究工房 近江町市場店), 27-2 Jikkenmachi, inside Omicho Market on its southwest edge ([Google Maps](https://maps.app.goo.gl/epQNdzvxeRLiWYpw7)). Its only Kanazawa branch, opened 2023; ~4.4–4.5 stars across several hundred reviews. Counter seats, and a line by the lunch rush, so be there at 11:00. **Hours: 10:00 AM–3:30 PM and 6:00–10:00 PM, closed Wednesdays.** Cards OK. How to order:
1. **Curry:** **gold Kanazawa curry** (the classic thick roux, get this one), black keema with green chili, or red butter chicken. Spice level 1–10
2. **Rice:** white, brown or turmeric. The curry is the same amount whatever the rice size, so the small size is enough
3. **Toppings** (~¥150–550 each, 20+): for the classic Kanazawa plate get a **tonkatsu** (¥400), or the **Noto pork cutlet** (¥550, local pork). Lotus root and sweet potato are local autumn vegetables too

Dan has the classic version at Turban Curry on [Nov 4](./2026-11-04.md), so this is the modern take. If you also want seafood, snack from the stall grills afterward, or have seafood at Saturday's crab lunch in the same market.

**Other options:**
- **Champion Curry, Omicho branch** (カレーのチャンピオン近江町店), B1 of Omicho Ichiba-kan: the original Kanazawa-curry chain, fast counter food, no line usually. 11:00 AM–3:30 PM daily. Backup if the Lab's line is long

*Kaisendon:*
1. **Sensai Enishi (鮮彩えにし)**: run by one of the market's fish stalls. Get the signature **nodoguro hitsumabushi don**: blackthroat seaperch over rice, flecked with gold leaf. Eat it like Nagoya hitsumabushi, plain first, then with dashi poured over. Fri 11:00 AM–2:30 PM and 5:00–8:00 PM, closed Sundays
2. **Inoya (井ノ弥)**: said to be where Omicho's kaisendon started. 40+ bowls, big portions, so all three can order different ones. Weekdays 10:00 AM–3:00 PM (weekends from 9:30). It's popular, and the line can start before it opens
- **Fallbacks:** **Uouma (魚旨)**, kaisendon and nigiri, 11:00 AM–4:00 PM daily. **Morimori Sushi**, conveyor belt, 8:00 AM–4:00 PM, but expect a long wait unless you're in the ticket queue before ~10:45
- **What to order:** **nodoguro**, **amaebi** and **gasu-ebi** (Kanazawa's shrimp, at its peak Sep–Nov; gasu-ebi rarely leaves the area), and buri (yellowtail) as winter starts

## Things to look for at Omicho
- **Snack while walking the aisles:** grilled scallops or oysters from the stall grills, **seafood croquettes**, and the free samples many fish stalls hand out
- **Autumn local produce:** **Gorojima Kintoki** sweet potato and **Kaga lotus root** (look for them as croquettes or snacks), plus **kabura-zushi** (yellowtail pressed between turnip). Stalls start making kabura-zushi in November, so it's hit or miss
- **Scout tomorrow's crab:** stop at **Kawaki Shoten** (9:00 AM–3:00 PM) and ask what they'll have Saturday and when it'll be out, so the Nov 7 lunch goes quickly
- **Small souvenirs that travel well:** **Kaga bocha** (roasted-stalk tea, light and cheap), **jiro-ame** (the traditional malt-syrup candy; also as soft serve), Kanazawa sweets, and dried or packaged seafood snacks. Skip raw or fresh seafood: it won't last, and US customs won't let it in
- **Ichihime Shrine**: a tiny shrine tucked inside the market
- **Bring cash.** Many stalls don't take cards. Lots of stalls close on Wednesdays, but Friday is a normal day

**Jibu-ni backup:** **Genzaemon** (Katamachi, izakaya with kamo jibu-ni and ~30 local sakes; 5:00–11:00 PM, closed Mon). Splurge option: **Zeniya** (Katamachi, refined Kaga cuisine, 5:30–10:00 PM, closed Sun, reserve; cancellation needs 7 business days' notice). Some places use chicken instead of duck, so ask.
